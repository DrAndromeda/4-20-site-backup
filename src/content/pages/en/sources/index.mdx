This page describes how the content of 4-20.com.ua is researched, written and corrected, and where to find the organisations we rely on most. It exists so that readers can judge for themselves how far to trust what they read here.

## Editorial principles

**Educational, neutral and honest about uncertainty.** The site explains what cannabis is and what research says about it. It does not encourage use, does not promote products and avoids hype and unsupported claims. Where the evidence is limited, pages say so in plain words instead of rounding up to a confident statement.

Each page begins with a direct answer, cites its sources and shows the date it was last updated. Articles are written natively in each language for local readers, not mechanically translated, and the same facts are checked across all language versions.

## How claims are sourced

**Facts and figures are tied to named, checkable sources.** Preference goes to public-health agencies, international organisations, regulators, scientific databases and peer-reviewed literature. Numbers such as boiling points or molar masses come from chemical reference databases. Where a statement would be impossible to verify, we leave it out or say that evidence is limited. We do not cite anonymous forums, vendors or marketing copy as evidence.

## Evidence hierarchy

**Not all evidence is equal, and the site tries to label it.** In descending order of reliability for a question about effects in people:

1. Systematic reviews and meta-analyses of well-conducted trials.
2. Randomised controlled trials.
3. Observational studies, such as cohort and case-control studies.
4. Case reports and expert opinion.
5. Preclinical research in cells and animals, which can suggest hypotheses but does not show that something works in humans.

When a page talks about a terpene or cannabinoid whose evidence is mostly preclinical, it is described as such. Approved drugs are named as approved drugs; that is different from a plant product being said to treat a condition.

## Reference organisations

The following bodies are the backbone of our references. Links go to their main landing pages:

- [NIDA](https://nida.nih.gov/research-topics/cannabis-marijuana), the U.S. National Institute on Drug Abuse, for research summaries on cannabis use and health effects.
- [NCCIH](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/cannabis-marijuana-and-cannabinoids-what-you-need-to-know), the U.S. National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health, for evidence on medical uses.
- [EUDA](https://www.euda.europa.eu/topics/cannabis_en), the European Union Drugs Agency, for European data and policy overviews.
- [WHO](https://www.who.int/teams/mental-health-and-substance-use/alcohol-drugs-and-addictive-behaviours/drugs-psychoactive/cannabis), the World Health Organization, for global health perspectives.
- [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/what-you-need-know-and-what-were-working-find-out-about-products-containing-cannabis-or-cannabis), the U.S. Food and Drug Administration, for approved drugs and product-safety information.
- [PubChem](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/), the chemical database of the U.S. National Library of Medicine, for structures and properties of compounds.
- [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/), the biomedical literature database, for peer-reviewed studies.

For legal topics in Ukraine, the primary sources are the Verkhovna Rada portal, [zakon.rada.gov.ua](https://zakon.rada.gov.ua/), and the [Ministry of Health](https://moz.gov.ua/).

## Corrections and updates

**We correct errors openly and update pages when evidence or law changes.** Each page displays an update date. If a statement is shown to be inaccurate, we revise it and change the date; substantive changes to health or legal statements are reviewed against the underlying sources again. If you notice an error, a broken link or an outdated claim, please tell us through the contact details provided on the site and, where possible, include a link to the source that shows the problem.
