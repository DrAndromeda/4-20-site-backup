This page is a general educational overview and is not legal advice. Drug laws change, are applied differently in practice and depend on the circumstances of each case. Anyone who needs to know their legal position should consult a qualified Ukrainian lawyer and read the current text of the law on the official portal.

## General position

**Cannabis is a controlled substance in Ukraine, and unauthorised activity involving it is prohibited.** Ukraine is a party to the UN drug control conventions, and its domestic legislation places cannabis and its preparations among narcotic substances whose circulation is restricted to licensed medical, scientific or similar uses. Lists of narcotic drugs, psychotropic substances and precursors are approved by the government, and they are the legal reference for what counts as a controlled substance.

In practice, this means that growing, producing, preparing, acquiring, storing, transporting and supplying cannabis without authorisation are all legally problematic. The seriousness of the consequences varies by the activity and by the quantity.

## Administrative versus criminal liability

**Ukrainian law distinguishes between small quantities, which are dealt with under administrative law, and larger quantities, which fall under the Criminal Code.** The Code of Administrative Offences covers unlawful production, acquisition, storage, transport or shipment of narcotic substances without the purpose of sale in small amounts. The Criminal Code addresses the same conduct in larger amounts, as well as sale and other forms of supply, which are treated far more seriously.

Which category applies is decided by the weight of the substance, according to official tables of small, large and especially large amounts of narcotic drugs. Those tables are approved by the health authorities and have been revised over the years. For that reason this page deliberately does not quote numbers: a figure printed here could become outdated or be misapplied. The current thresholds are published on the Verkhovna Rada portal, [zakon.rada.gov.ua](https://zakon.rada.gov.ua/), and the Ministry of Health website.

Other factors can also matter, such as the place where an act happened, the involvement of minors, and whether a person drove while impaired.

## Medical cannabis reform

**In late 2024 the Verkhovna Rada adopted a law to regulate the circulation of cannabis for medical, scientific and industrial purposes.** The reform was widely discussed in Ukraine and was linked to the needs of patients and to the development of regulated production. As of this page's last update, the details depend on secondary legislation and implementing regulations from the government and the Ministry of Health, including rules on licensing, prescribing, quality control and supply.

Because implementation is staged, readers should not assume that the adoption of a law means that products are already available or that any individual may use cannabis legally. What exactly is permitted, to whom and under which conditions must be checked on the official sources. Note too that medical frameworks generally involve physician prescription and licensed pharmacies, not self-supply. A wider look at what research says about therapeutic use is on the [medical overview](/en/medical/).

## Why the details are easy to get wrong

**Legal texts about narcotic substances are technical, and summaries easily become outdated.** Terms such as "small amount" have precise legal meanings that depend on the specific substance, its form and its weight, and the way a substance is measured can itself matter. Court decisions also shape how provisions are applied. In addition, the reform of medical, scientific and industrial circulation changes the surrounding framework step by step, so older articles, forum posts and even some news reports may describe a situation that no longer exists. Reading the current consolidated text on the official portal, and checking its date, is the only dependable approach.

## Practical points for readers

- Laws can be amended, and bylaws, court practice and enforcement can differ from the text; always verify.
- Information on foreign laws does not apply in Ukraine, even for Ukrainian citizens who return home.
- Travelling with cannabis products across borders is illegal in most countries, even where the product is legal at the origin.
- If you or someone you know is facing a legal situation, a lawyer is the right source of advice, not an educational website.

## Risks and who should be careful

Legal exposure can be serious, with effects on employment, travel and driving licences. Health risks are covered in [dosing and safety](/en/consumption/dosing-and-safety/), including the rule that no one should drive after using cannabis. People who feel their use is becoming a problem can speak to a doctor or an addiction service.

## Where to verify

The authoritative sources are the Verkhovna Rada legal portal [zakon.rada.gov.ua](https://zakon.rada.gov.ua/) for the text of laws and the [Ministry of Health](https://moz.gov.ua/) for lists, tables and regulations. For a European comparison, the EUDA publishes overviews of national drug laws. This site is not a source of legal information, and its [legal information](/en/legal/) page explains its limits.
